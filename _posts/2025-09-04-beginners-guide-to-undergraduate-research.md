# Starting Your Research Journey as an Undergraduate

Research as an idea has always fascinated me. As a child, I observed my father and his work as a peptide chemist. I learnt from him how simple changes in large biomolecules could help produce life-saving drugs. I was amazed by how science and technology could come together and work for the betterment of human lives.  

Time progressed, and I found myself at the doorstep of the **National Institute of Technology Tiruchirappalli** as a freshman in 2021. Like any other freshman, I was amazed by the multitude of career opportunities that were presented before me. I must admit that I was equally overwhelmed as I was astonished by this abundance.  

It was around this time that I had the chance to speak to **Ms. Sujana Ramesh** through a friend. Sujana told me about her first research experience under a professor at NIT Tiruchirappalli. It felt to me that the stars were aligning, and I decided that I too, wanted to try my hand at research.  

While the decision was made almost spontaneously, what followed was weeks or even months of preparation before the actual research started. Through this article, I will try to talk about some of the most common doubts that undergraduate students have before diving into research. Since I pursued my Bachelor’s in India, all the information below may hold greater relevance in the Indian context.

---

## Who should pursue research during their undergraduate study?

**Anyone!** In fact, I’d argue that everyone should. Professors are often very happy to host undergraduate students in their laboratories.  

As an undergrad, keep your mind open to different ideas. Top institutes in India like the **IITs, IISc and top NITs** have world-class research happening. Professors at these institutes are often alumni of prestigious universities from around the world.  

Working with these researchers will:
- Expose you to the latest advancements in the industry.  
- Give you the chance to network with other researchers in the lab.  
- Help you form meaningful connections and gain mentorship.  

This can be crucial if you plan to pursue your **Master’s or PhD** at a later point.

---

## When is it a good time to start my research journey?

It would be ideal to start your research journey **from the end of your third semester**.  

This is particularly true for students who aim to study core engineering subjects. The first year of engineering focuses mainly on mathematics and general science. It is only from the third semester that students are exposed to core engineering subjects in their chosen discipline.  

This can be a crucial semester to identify if you want to pursue a career in your chosen field or pivot to something different.

---

## How to identify which area to pursue research in?

This is an important question.  

Some people find their calling in their chosen engineering major. Others may be interested in subjects they were exposed to through electives or student-run activities.  

For example, I found my calling in **robotics** through the robotics club at my college.  

👉 My suggestion: pick any discipline and get started. As you spend more time studying a subject, you will naturally know whether you want to pursue it or not.  

---

## How to prepare for my first research project?

Once you have identified your area of interest, you have completed **step zero in research**.  

Before you approach a professor:  
- Work on smaller projects in related domains to build fundamentals.  
- Start with professors in your home institution before reaching out to others.  

Each professor typically has a website listing their research interests and projects. Go through them and find what part resonates with you.  

If it is a professor at your university, reach out to them in person.  
- Talk about why you want to work with them.  
- Explain how you would like to contribute.  

---

## How can I work with professors from other universities?

The simplest way is to **write an email**. Here are some tips:  

- **Prepare your resume** beforehand. Use a clean template (Abey’s and Jake’s are my favourites). Highlight relevant projects.  
- **Keep your email short and simple.**  
  - Subject line: neat and clear.  
  - Introduce yourself and explain how your research interests align with theirs.  
  - Briefly describe your projects and what you’d like to work on.  
  - Mention the duration you are willing to commit.  

Most importantly, your email should reflect your **willingness to learn and contribute**.  

---

## I have written my email. What can I do while I wait for a reply?

Most professors conduct simple interviews based on your previous coursework and resume. These are mainly to assess your aptitude and learning curve.  

Some may also give you small assignments to test your programming skills.  

At your first research experience:  
- Communicate your goals clearly with your advisor.  
- If you aim to publish a paper, let your advisor know and ask how to work towards it.  
- Ask for a **mentor in the lab** whom you can approach for doubts.  
- Send **weekly updates** to your professor about your work.  
- If possible, schedule regular meetings with your mentor/professor, present your progress, and seek feedback.
